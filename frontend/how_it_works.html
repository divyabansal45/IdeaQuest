How IdeaQuest works

Seven simple steps take your idea from a few lines of text to a list of similar patents and suggestions.

1. Clean the text

Your idea becomes lowercase words. Filler words such as the, and and with are removed, and so are generic patent words such as method and apparatus.

Word endings are cut, so charging, charges and charged become one word.

2. Group synonyms

A small word list groups words that mean the same thing. Car, automobile and motorcar all become vehicle, so they count as one idea. Without this, they would be treated as unrelated words.

3. Find related concepts

Some concepts are different but often used together, such as solar and battery. IdeaQuest keeps a map of such links and follows it from the concepts in your idea. It lists the closest ones first, then ones further away, and shows one long chain of connected ideas.

4. Turn words into numbers

The words are counted, and each word gets a weight. Rare, specific words get a high weight. Common words get a low weight. This is called TF-IDF.

TF = times the word appears ÷ total words IDF = log( total patents ÷ patents containing the word ) TF-IDF = TF × IDF

5. Find similar patents

Your idea and every patent become lists of weights. Cosine similarity measures how close two lists are, from 0 (nothing in common) to 1 (identical). The five highest scores are shown.

cosine = (A · B) ÷ ( |A| × |B| )

6. Compare features

Each feature of your idea gets a match score from 0 to 1. It is the share of the similar patents, weighted by their similarity, that contain the feature.

The label comes from fuzzy logic. A score can belong a little to two labels at once, and the label with the higher degree wins.

LabelScore range
Not foundFull up to a score of 0.1, then falls to 0 at 0.3
Partly coveredRises from 0.1, peaks at 0.4, falls to 0 at 0.7
Already existsRises from 0.4 and is full from 0.7

7. Suggest improvements

A set of IF-THEN rules looks at the labels, the similarity scores and the words that similar patents share. Every rule that applies becomes a suggestion, and the "Why?" under it names the rule.

Example: IF a word appears in at least 2 similar patents AND is missing from your idea, THEN suggest adding it.

Know the limits

  • Your idea is not saved. Results are guidance, not legal advice.
  • The patent data and the rules are made by people, so they can miss things.
  • Older patents that have only a title give rough results.
  • Every step uses fixed formulas and written rules, so the same idea always gives the same result.
